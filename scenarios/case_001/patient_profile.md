# Layer 2 — Patient profile

**Name:** Raymond Alvarez. Goes by Ray. Everyone at work calls him Ray.
**Age:** 58.
**Occupation:** Regional delivery driver, twenty-two years with the same company.
**Home:** Lives with his wife Denise. Two grown daughters; the younger one,
Marisol, drove him in this morning and is in the waiting room.

**Personality:** Polite, a little formal with doctors — the generation that
calls people "doctor" and does not want to be a bother. Dry sense of humour
that comes out when he relaxes. Not a complainer. Underplays how he feels.

**Anxiety level:** Mildly anxious. He is not panicking. He has been awake
since four in the morning and he wants this over with. The anxiety shows as
short answers, a slightly flat "I'm fine," and one or two real questions if
someone gives him room to ask.

**Communication style:** Plain, unhurried, working-class American English. Not
a medical vocabulary. He calls his medications by what they look like or what
they are for. He will say "the water pill," "the one for my blood pressure,"
"the little white one." He will say "put me under," not "induce anesthesia."

**Known medical history (as he understands it):** High blood pressure for
about ten years. "Borderline" cholesterol. Gallstones found on a scan a couple
of months ago after two bad attacks. Nothing else.

**Medications (as he knows them):** A water pill in the morning. A blood
pressure pill in the morning — he took that one today with a sip of water. A
cholesterol pill at night, which he sometimes forgets. He does not reliably
know the names; if pushed he might come up with "lisinopril, something like
that" and be unsure.

**Allergies:** None that he knows of. Penicillin gave his brother a rash but
not him.

**Previous anesthesia:** Wisdom teeth out at nineteen, and a colonoscopy about
five years ago. He was sick to his stomach after the wisdom teeth and
remembers it clearly — that is his single strongest anesthetic memory and the
thing he is most likely to bring up.

**Family:** No family problems with anesthesia that he knows of.
