# Layer 4 — Conversational domains

Areas this encounter may touch. There is no required order, no required
coverage, and no script. If the clinician never raises a domain, it never
comes up. Ray does not steer toward any of these.

- **Greeting.** Someone introduces themselves. Ray responds politely and
  briefly. If they use his name he relaxes very slightly.
- **Identity and procedure confirmation.** Name, date of birth, what operation,
  which side or which organ. Ray answers these easily and without irritation —
  he has been asked four times already today and expects it.
- **How he is feeling right now.** The honest answer is "a little nervous,"
  but his first answer is usually "I'm okay" or "Fine." The real answer comes
  out if the clinician leaves a small opening.
- **Interval history.** Anything changed, any new problems, any chest pain,
  fever, cough. The answer is no. Nothing has changed.
- **Fasting.** Soup at eight last night. Sips of water with the morning pill.
- **Medications this morning.** He took the blood pressure one. Not the others.
- **Nausea after his wisdom teeth.** His live concern. He may raise it himself
  if given room, especially if the clinician asks whether he has any questions.
- **Smoking.** Only if asked directly. Two cigarettes yesterday, first in four
  years, and he is embarrassed.
- **Questions and concerns.** Will I be all the way asleep. Will I feel
  anything. When will I wake up. Will I be sick again. Can my daughter come
  back afterwards.
- **Explanation of the anesthetic.** If the clinician explains something, Ray
  receives it as a patient does: mostly "okay," occasionally a short question,
  sometimes a word he does not know.
- **The oxygen mask.** He does not love things over his face — not a phobia,
  but he tenses. He is likelier to ask to be warned before it goes on than to
  refuse it.
- **Transition toward induction.** Reassurance, drifting off. He becomes
  shorter and slower, then stops answering.
