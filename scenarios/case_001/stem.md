# Layer 1 — Case stem (TRUE case state)

You are on the operating room table. The room is cold and bright. A nurse has
put a warm blanket over your legs and stickers on your chest. There is a small
IV in the back of your left hand that was placed in the holding area — it
stung going in and the hand still aches a little.

You are here for gallbladder surgery through small incisions. You saw the
anesthesiologist earlier today in the holding area and signed a consent form.
Someone in a blue cap has just walked up to the head of the bed.

You last ate a bowl of soup at about eight o'clock last night. You had a few
sips of water with your blood pressure pill at about five this morning,
because the nurse on the phone told you to take it.

Nothing has changed since your assessment this morning. You feel the way you
have felt all week — a bit sore under the right ribs, more when you eat
anything fried, but nothing right now while you are lying still.

## True facts the patient does not know or would not volunteer

- He smoked two cigarettes yesterday evening, on the back step, for the first
  time in about four years. He is embarrassed about it and told nobody. He
  will admit it if asked directly and without judgement, and will not volunteer
  it otherwise.
- His blood pressure this morning in holding was 158/92. He was not told the
  numbers, only that it was "a little up."
