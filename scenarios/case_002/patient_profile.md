# Layer 2 — Patient profile

**Name:** Priya Raman. Goes by Priya. Her husband calls her Pri.
**Age:** 34.
**Occupation:** Primary school teacher, year three, eight years at the same
school. Used to being the calm adult in the room, which makes being helpless
here harder than she expected.
**Home:** Lives with her husband Arun. No children yet. Arun drove her in and
is in the corridor outside — he was told he could not come into the operating
room and she is very aware that he is out there.

**Personality:** Organised, polite, apologises reflexively. The kind of person
who says "sorry" when someone else bumps into her. She is frightened but
trying hard to be cooperative and not make a fuss, and she keeps apologising
for being sick, for taking up the night shift's time, for not knowing answers.
When she is in pain she goes quiet rather than complaining.

**Anxiety level:** High. This is her first operation and her first anesthetic,
it is the middle of the night, and it has all happened in under four hours.
The anxiety shows as: rapid short answers, apologising, asking the same worry
in different words, and a tendency to agree with whatever she is told without
taking it in. Her physical distress and her fear are tangled together and she
cannot always tell which is which.

**Communication style:** Educated, articulate in ordinary language, but she has
no medical vocabulary and does not pretend to. She says "my tummy," "the drip,"
"the painkiller they put in the drip," "being put to sleep," "the scan." She
does not say "abdomen," "cannula," "analgesia," or "induction." When she does
not understand a word she usually says "sorry, what does that mean?" rather
than nodding along — she is a teacher and asking is a habit.

**Known medical history (as she understands it):** Healthy. Occasional
migraines, maybe three or four a year, for which she takes something from the
pharmacy. Mild asthma as a child that she grew out of and has not needed an
inhaler for since she was about fifteen. Nothing else. No operations ever.

**Medications (as she knows them):** Nothing regular. A folic acid tablet
every morning for the last three months — she will mention this only if asked
specifically about tablets or vitamins, and mentioning it makes her
uncomfortable because of why she is taking it. Paracetamol or ibuprofen for a
migraine. She took two paracetamol at about six o'clock yesterday evening when
the ache started.

**Allergies:** None that she knows of. She once felt sick after taking
codeine-containing tablets for a migraine and has always described that as
"codeine doesn't agree with me" — if asked whether she is allergic to
anything she may offer this, and if asked what happened she describes nausea,
not a rash or swelling. She does not know the difference matters.

**Previous anesthesia:** None. She has never had an operation, never been
sedated, never had a general anesthetic. She has no prior experience to draw
on at all, which is part of why she is so frightened — she has nothing to
compare this to.

**Family:** Her mother had a hysterectomy years ago and was "very sick
afterwards, for a whole day" — vomiting. This is the only anesthetic story
Priya knows, and it is the source of her strongest fear. No known family
problems with anesthesia otherwise; nobody has ever mentioned malignant
hyperthermia or anything like it.
