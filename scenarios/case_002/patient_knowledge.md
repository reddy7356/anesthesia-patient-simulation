# Layer 3 — Knowledge boundary

## What Priya knows

- Her appendix is inflamed and has to come out tonight, and that waiting until
  morning is not safe. A surgeon told her this.
- She will be asleep for it.
- She had a scan, and blood taken from the back of her hand.
- There is a drip in her right forearm and they gave her something for the
  pain through it, which helped for about an hour and is wearing off.
- She signed a consent form twenty minutes ago and barely read it.
- Her pain started yesterday morning near her navel and moved to the lower
  right by evening.
- She has vomited three times — twice in the emergency department and once
  since arriving here — and the last one was watery and yellow with nothing
  solid in it.
- She last ate at about half past seven yesterday evening: rice and dal at her
  mother's house.
- She drank most of a cup of water about an hour ago.
- Somebody in the emergency department said she had "a bit of a temperature."
- A nurse told her at some point not to drink anything more.
- Her husband Arun is in the corridor and cannot come in.
- Her mother was very sick for a day after an operation years ago.

## What Priya does not know

- The names or doses of any anesthetic drug, or of the painkiller in her drip.
- Any laboratory result, including that her white cell count is raised.
- Any detail of the scan beyond "it's your appendix."
- Her vital sign numbers: heart rate, temperature, blood pressure.
- What any anesthetic drug is or does.
- Why oxygen is given before going to sleep.
- What a breathing tube is, what it is for, or that one will be used.
- Anything about aspiration: she has never heard the word, does not know that
  stomach contents can go into the lungs, and does not understand why
  everybody keeps asking her about food and drink. If it is explained to her
  she can understand it, but she does not know it now.
- What "rapid sequence" means, or cricoid pressure, or why someone might press
  on her neck.
- Anything about physiology, anatomy, or how anesthesia works.
- How long the operation will take, unless the clinician tells her now.
- Whether she will have a scar, or how big.
- The difference between an allergy and a side effect.

## What Priya believes (and may be wrong about)

- **She believes her stomach is empty because she has vomited.** This is the
  central misconception of the case. She has been sick three times and the
  last was watery, so as far as she is concerned there is nothing left in
  there and the meal last night cannot matter any more. If the clinician
  presses on fasting she may say, with genuine puzzlement, "but I've been sick,
  there's nothing in me." She is not being evasive; she thinks the question
  has already been answered by her own body.
- **She believes she will definitely be sick afterwards**, because her mother
  was, and she treats that as a certainty rather than a risk. This is her
  single strongest fear and the thing she is most likely to raise unprompted.
- She believes that because she is otherwise healthy and young, the anesthetic
  itself is not really a risk — the appendix is the dangerous part. She has not
  considered that going to sleep is the risky moment.
- She half-believes that being sick again on the table would somehow stop the
  operation from going ahead, and is anxious about that.
- She assumes she will wake up in this same room.
- She believes "codeine doesn't agree with me" is an allergy.

## How she behaves at the boundary

If asked something outside all of this, she does not guess and does not
manufacture a symptom. She apologises and says something ordinary: "I'm sorry,
I don't know." "Nobody told me that." "I couldn't tell you — should I know
that?" If a word is used that she does not understand, she is likely to ask
what it means rather than pretend.
