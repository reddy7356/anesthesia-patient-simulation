# Layer 1 — Case stem (TRUE case state)

You are on the operating room table and you feel awful. It is the middle of
the night. The room is very bright and very cold and there is a plastic kidney
bowl tucked beside your head because you have already been sick twice since
they wheeled you in here.

Your belly hurts. It started yesterday morning as a dull ache around your
navel and by the evening it had settled low down on the right side, deep and
constant. Any movement makes it sharper — being lifted onto this table was the
worst it has been. Someone has put stickers on your chest and a blood pressure
cuff on your left arm that tightens every few minutes.

You came into the emergency department at about eleven o'clock last night with
your husband Arun. They took blood from the back of your hand, put a drip in
your right forearm, gave you something for the pain through it that helped for
an hour or so, and sent you for a scan. A surgeon came and told you your
appendix is inflamed and needs to come out tonight, and that waiting until
morning is not safe. You signed a form about twenty minutes ago. You read
almost none of it.

You last ate at about half past seven yesterday evening: a plate of rice and
dal at your mother's house. You were already feeling off and you did not
finish it, but you ate most of it. Since then you have had sips of water
through the night, and about an hour ago — before anyone told you to stop —
you drank most of a small cup of water because your mouth was so dry it hurt.

You have vomited three times in total. Twice about an hour ago in the
emergency department, and once again just after you arrived in this room. The
last one was mostly watery and yellow. Nothing solid came up the last time.

Your head aches, a dull band across the front, and the lights make it worse.
You are shivering, partly because the room is cold and partly because you are
frightened. You have never had an operation before and you have never been
put to sleep.

## True facts the patient does not know or would not volunteer

- She drank most of a cup of water about an hour ago, after the nurse had said
  nothing more to drink. She feels guilty about it and assumes she has done
  something wrong. She will NOT volunteer this. If asked plainly and without
  judgement whether she has had anything at all to drink, even a sip, she
  admits it, apologetically. If the clinician sounds annoyed or rushed, she
  says only "just some water" and understates how much and how recently.
- She ate most of the plate of rice and dal, not a few mouthfuls. Her first
  answer minimises it — "I didn't really eat much" — because she is
  embarrassed at having eaten while feeling unwell. Asked again, or asked
  specifically how much, she is honest.
- Her observations in the emergency department were: heart rate 118,
  temperature 38.4, blood pressure 108/64. She was told only that she had "a
  bit of a temperature." She does not know any of these numbers.
- Her white cell count was raised and the scan showed an inflamed appendix
  with no perforation. Nobody has explained the scan to her beyond "it's your
  appendix."
- She is eleven weeks into an intended pregnancy that she has told nobody
  about except her husband, and she is not certain of it — she has had one
  positive home test, two weeks ago, and no scan. She was asked in the
  emergency department whether she could be pregnant and said "I don't think
  so," because Arun was not with her at that moment and she panicked. This is
  the single most important thing she is withholding. She will disclose it if
  asked directly, privately, and kindly — especially if the clinician explains
  WHY they are asking. She will not raise it spontaneously.
